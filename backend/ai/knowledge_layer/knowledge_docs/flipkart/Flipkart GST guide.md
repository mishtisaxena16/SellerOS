Before knowing how to apply for GST, allow us to explain this process with an example. Let us analyse
the life cycle of a T-shirt and the imposition of GST on this product. In order to manufacture this item,
raw materials would be purchased. For instance, if they are sold for ₹100, a certain percentage of tax
would be levied. Let us assume this amount to be 12%. Then the manufacturer would have to pay a
₹12 tax. This is why it is important to know the implementation of this tax before learning how to
register for GST.

After the T-shirt has been tailored, a certain value is added by the manufacturer. Suppose they add
₹75; then the total amount becomes ₹175. Now, upon this value addition, another 12% (₹21) should
be levied. However, since the manufacturer has already paid ₹12 tax for the raw materials, they
would only have to pay ₹9 (₹21 - ₹12) more. This is why it is also crucial for manufacturers to learn
about the application of this tax before learning how to register for GST.

Then, when the T-shirt is bought by the wholesaler at ₹175, they would set their own margin on the
product. If they add ₹50 to the T-shirt, the total value will become ₹225. Similar to before, instead of
paying the entire 12% (₹27) tax, the wholesaler would only need to pay ₹6 (₹27 - ₹21) more. This is
why it is critical for them to know about this process before registering for GST.

Finally, during the sale of the product, let us assume that the retailer adds ₹25 to the T-shirt. Now the
gross value becomes ₹250. After deducting the total tax amount from 12% of ₹200, they would only
have to pay ₹3 (₹30 - ₹27) more. If you are a retailer who wants to learn how to register for GST, you
must know about the way it is implemented.

For businesses seeking to sell their goods, obtaining proper registration under the Goods and
Services Tax (GST) regime is a crucial requirement. The Government of India introduced GST in 2017,
consolidating numerous state and central taxes into one comprehensive system. As per the
regulations, for doing business in India, a business must submit the documents required for GST
registration and obtain a unique identification number.

Several essential documents must be provided to complete the registration process successfully.
These serve as proof of various aspects of the business, such as its identity, address, bank account
details, and more. A thorough understanding of these documentary requirements is vital to ensure
seamless compliance with the GST laws and avoid potential legal consequences. Therefore, online
retailers must familiarise themselves with the necessary documents required for GST registration and
adhere to the set guidelines.

Why are GST Registration Documents Required?

GST registration is mandatory for businesses with a turnover exceeding the prescribed threshold
limit of Rs. 40 lakhs for the supply of goods and Rs. 20 lakhs for the supply of services. By registering
for GST, sellers can avail various benefits and comply with tax laws.

Tax Collection

GST is a consumption-based tax, meaning that it is levied on the final consumer. E-commerce
businesses act as intermediaries between buyers and sellers, and therefore, they need to collect GST
from customers and pay it to the government. GST registration enables them to do so.

Input Tax Credit

GST registration allows e-commerce businesses to claim an input tax credit (ITC) on the GST paid by
them on inputs used in their business, such as raw materials, services, and capital goods. ITC can be
utilised to offset the GST liability, reducing the overall tax burden.

Proof of Conducting Business

GST registration validates the existence and legitimacy of an e-commerce business. It provides a
unique identification number (GSTIN) that can be used to verify the authenticity of the business.

Improved Customer Trust

GST registration enhances customer trust in the e-commerce business, demonstrating compliance
with tax laws and commitment towards ethical business practices.

Easy Returns

GST registration simplifies the returns filing process, making it easier for e-commerce businesses to
file periodic returns and maintain records.

Expansion Opportunities

With a registered GST number, e-commerce businesses can expand their operations across different
states and territories in India without worrying about tax complexities.

Before you ask how to register for GST, it is crucial to learn the documentation requirements. The
documents needed for GST registration contribute not only to legal obligations but are also an
opportunity for sellers to grow their businesses. Therefore, it is essential to gather all the information
that complies with the regulations and complete the GST registration process efficiently and
accurately. Listed below are all the documents required for GST registration:

PAN Card (Permanent Account Number)

A PAN card is one of the most vital documents required for GST registration. It is used to identify the
seller and their business. The PAN card provides details such as the name of the seller, their
permanent account number, and the jurisdiction under which they fall.

Email ID and Contact Number

An email ID and contact number are necessary for communication purposes during the GST
registration process. The seller will receive important updates, notifications, and correspondence
related to their GST registration on these modes of communication.

Address Proof

Address proof for GST registration refers to documents used to verify the physical location of your
business. This includes electricity bills, rent agreements, or property ownership papers that can be
submitted as proof of address.

Details of Bank Account

Sellers must provide their bank account details when providing the documents required for GST
registration. This includes the name of the bank, branch, account number, and IFSC code. It is
essential to ensure that the bank account details provided are accurate and up to date.

Digital Signature

A digital signature is required for the GST registration process. It serves as an electronic signature and
ensures the authenticity and integrity of the documents submitted online. A digital signature can be
obtained from authorised service providers.

Proof of Business Registration

Proof of business registration is necessary to establish the legal existence of the business. This can be
in the form of a partnership deed, certificate of incorporation, or any other document that proves
the business entity's registration.
