What is the Meaning of Stock Keeping Unit (SKU)?

SKU stands for Stock Keeping Unit, a distinct alphanumeric code assigned to a specific product for the
purpose of internal inventory tracking. This code is unique to each company and is different from
universal product codes (UPCs) that are standardised across different retailers for the same item. An
SKU typically includes a combination of characters (letters and numbers) and is designed to represent
key attributes of a product, such as its type, size, colour, or other relevant characteristics.

The primary function of a product SKU is to uniquely identify each item in a retailer's inventory,
making it easier to track the quantity of stock on hand, manage store layouts, and streamline the
order fulfilment process. For instance, an SKU might look something like 'SHRT-BLU-M' for a medium-
sized blue shirt, where each segment of the code provides specific information about the product.
This coding system allows retailers to efficiently manage their inventory, ensuring that they have the
right products in the right quantity at the right time. To sum up the definition of SKU, it plays an
integral part in the operations of retail businesses, simplifying the complex task of inventory
management.

How Stock Keeping Units Function in Business Operations

Creating SKUs

Assigning unique alphanumeric codes to each product is the first step in creating SKUs. This code
often reflects significant characteristics of the product, such as its category, size, colour, or style. For
example, a furniture store might use SKUs like 'SOFA-BLUE-L' for a large blue sofa. This distinguishes
it from other sofas and embeds vital product information within the seller SKU ID itself. By
standardising this process, businesses can systematically categorise their inventory, making it more
manageable.

Data Encoding

SKUs serve as compact repositories of crucial product information. This makes it easier for businesses
to access and analyse product details quickly. For instance, in a clothing store, the SKU could reveal
information about the item’s brand, size, colour, and type without needing to look up the product in
a database. This encoding of data into SKUs streamlines operations like restocking, identifying trends,
and making purchasing decisions.

Tracking of Inventory

SKU enables real-time tracking of inventory levels and movements, which is essential for maintaining
optimal stock levels. This tracking helps businesses avoid overstocking or understocking situations.
For example, a grocery store could use SKUs to monitor the turnover rate of perishable goods,
ensuring that stock is replenished just in time to meet customer demand while minimising waste.

Fulfilment of Orders

In the order fulfilment process, the Stock Keeping Unit or SKU plays a crucial role in ensuring
accuracy and efficiency. They enable quick identification and picking of items for orders, reducing the
chances of errors. For instance, in a warehouse, workers can quickly scan the SKUs to ensure the
right product is being packed and shipped. This speeds up the process and reduces errors in delivery.

Evaluation of Data

SKU facilitates comprehensive data collection, allowing businesses to analyse sales patterns,
inventory turnover, and customer preferences. This data can inform a range of strategic decisions like
adjusting pricing, planning marketing campaigns, and optimising the product range. For instance, if
you sell home appliances online, you can use SKU data to determine which gadgets or brands are
most popular among consumers, thereby optimising stock to focus more on these high-demand
electronics.

How to Create Relevant SKUs for Your Business

Now that you are aware of the SKU meaning in business, it is time to learn how to create relevant
SKUs for your brand. This involves a strategic approach to ensure that your SKUs are not only unique
but also informative and easy to understand. Here are the key steps:

Keep the SKUs as Small as Possible

Shorter SKUs are more manageable and less prone to errors. For example, a compact SKU like
'BK1002' for a specific book is easier to handle than a lengthy one like
'BOOK2022THRILLERHARDCOVER'. Short SKUs are quicker to read, easier to input, and reduce the
likelihood of mistyping.

Avoid the Use of Special Characters or Spaces

Avoiding special characters and spaces in SKUs eliminates confusion and technical issues. Characters
like '&', '%', or '#' can cause issues with inventory software and barcode scanners. For instance, using
'MEN-SHIRT-BLUE' could be problematic; instead, 'MENSHIRTBLU' would be more system-friendly.

Don't Start an SKU with a Zero

Many inventory systems interpret leading zeros as insignificant, potentially causing misinterpretation
of the SKU. For example, an SKU like '04567' might be read as '4567', leading to confusion. Start SKUs
with a non-zero digit or a letter to avoid this issue.

Create Distinct SKUs

Each SKU should be unique to prevent stock mismanagement. For instance, different colour variants
of the same product should have distinctly different SKUs, like 'SHIRTBLU01' and 'SHIRTGRN01', to
avoid confusion between the blue and green variants.

Avoid the Use of Product Titles in the SKUs

Using full product titles in SKUs can make them unnecessarily long and complex. Instead, use
abbreviations or codes that capture the essence of the product. For example, instead of
'BlackMensLeatherJacketLarge', a more concise SKU would be 'BLKMLJKT-L'.

Challenges in Implementing SKUs

Incorporating SKUs is a critical step for efficient inventory management. However, the process of
creating and maintaining a functional SKU system does possess some challenges. Here are some of
the common bottlenecks that need to be taken care of when implementing SKUs:

Regularly Updating SKUs:

Maintaining SKU IDs requires regular updates to ensure they remain relevant, especially when new
products are added, or existing products are modified. This continuous task can be time-consuming
and requires diligence. To overcome this challenge, you can opt for inventory management software
that automatically updates SKUs when changes occur, such as new product additions or
modifications.

Errors Made by People:

Human error in creating or handling Stock Keeping Units can lead to inventory discrepancies.
Mislabeling, typos, or incorrect data entry can cause significant issues in inventory tracking and order
fulfilment. To eliminate these concerns, you can implement staff training programs, introduce
double-checking protocols, and use barcode scanning for accuracy.

Difficulty in Creating Unique SKUs:

Designing an effective SKU system could be particularly challenging for businesses with diverse and
extensive inventories. Determining a system that is both comprehensive and manageable requires
careful planning and understanding of the inventory. Seek expert advice, use modular systems, and
establish standard criteria for SKU generation to tackle this obstacle.

Implementation Cost:

The cost of setting up and maintaining a Stock Keeping Unit system could be significant. This includes
costs for software, potential hardware, like scanners, and training staff on the new system. Perform
cost-benefit analyses, adopt phased implementation, and explore affordable or open-source
software options—this will help in mitigating upfront expenses and maximising the return on
investment over time.

Integration with Different Systems:

Ensuring that the SKU code works seamlessly with other business systems, such as accounting
software, e-commerce platforms, and supply chain management tools, can be complex. This
integration is crucial for the smooth functioning of the business but may require technical expertise.
Addressing this challenge involves ensuring SKU system compatibility and considering cloud-based
solutions for better adaptability.
