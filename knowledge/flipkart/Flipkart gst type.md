Insights on GST and Types of GST in India

GST (Goods and Services Tax) has revolutionised the tax framework in India, simplifying the complex
tax system into a more uniform structure. Here‘s a breakdown of the four main GST types and
percentages each tailored for different aspects of transactions within the country.

Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST)

This refers to a tax on the movement of goods and services between different states, and it's
collected by the central government. IGST simplifies the tax structure across state lines, ensuring a
seamless flow of tax credits and avoiding double taxation. The total tax amount collected under IGST
is eventually divided between the originating and destination states, allowing fair revenue
distribution.

Example: IGST

A marketplace facilitates the sale of electronic goods from a seller in Uttar Pradesh to a customer in
Tamil Nadu. IGST is charged on this inter-state transaction. The IGST collected is divided between the
Central Government and Tamil Nadu, making sure that the tax revenue helps the state where the
goods or services are consumed.

State Goods and Services Tax (SGST)

Imposed on intra-state transactions where both the seller and buyer are within the same state, SGST
revenue goes directly to the state government. This tax plays a crucial role in state finances,
supporting local infrastructure, health, and education initiatives. Interestingly, union territories like
Delhi and Puducherry also collect SGST due to their unique status of having their own legislative
assemblies.

Example: SGST

A seller based in Kolkata sells clothes online to a customer who is also in Kolkata. The transaction
attracts SGST, which is collected by the West Bengal government.

Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST)

This tax is also levied on intra-state supplies but is collected by the Central Government. It works
alongside SGST, and both taxes are applied to a transaction within a state. This helps to make sure
that the money collected from these taxes is distributed between the state and central governments.

Example: CGST

A Bengaluru-based electronics retailer sells a smartphone to a customer within Bengaluru. This
transaction incurs CGST, which is collected by the Central Government.

Union Territory Goods and Services Tax (UTGST)

UTGST is one of the types of GST that is applied to transactions within union territories that do not
have a legislative assembly. The money collected through UTGST supports the government of the
union territory, helping to make sure these areas are well connected with the national financial
system.

Example: UTGST

A furniture store in Chandigarh sells a sofa to a customer also in Chandigarh. As Chandigarh is a
Union Territory without its own legislative assembly, this transaction attracts both UTGST and CGST,
with the collected UTGST going to the Chandigarh administration.

A Few GST Rules That Apply to Online Sellers in India

1. Invoicing Rule

Proper invoicing is the backbone of GST compliance. Every online seller must issue GST-compliant
invoices that include details like GSTIN, description of goods/services, total amount, and GST rate
applied. This not only helps in tax filing but also aids in claiming input tax credit.

2. GST Return Filing

GST returns must be filed monthly, quarterly, or annually based on your turnover and the scheme
you opt for. Regular filing ensures compliance and helps avoid penalties. Using automated tools can
make this process easier, reducing the scope of errors. Online sellers must file various GST returns,
similar to offline businesses. Key forms include:

2.1 GSTR-1

This form captures all outward supplies or sales and needs to be filed monthly or quarterly. The filing
frequency depends on your annual aggregate turnover and whether you've opted for the QRMP
scheme, which allows quarterly filings for businesses with turnover up to Rs. 5 Crore .

2.2 GSTR-3B

A consolidated summary of inward and outward supplies that must be filed monthly by all GST
registered taxpayers. It’s important to file, even if there are no transactions (Nil returns), to avoid
penalties .

2.3 GSTR-4

This return is for those under the Composition Scheme, which offers simpler compliance for smaller
businesses with an annual turnover of up to ₹1.5 crore. It's filed annually, and it covers all
transactions for the year.

2.4 GSTR-8

E-commerce operators who collect tax at source must file this monthly. It reports the amount of TCS
collected and paid to the government.

2.5 GSTR-9

An annual return that consolidates the information from the monthly and quarterly returns filed
throughout the year. It’s a comprehensive overview of a company’s GST liabilities.

Benefits of Various Types of GST for Online Sellers

This tax system has lots of benefits for businesses, making things easier and opening up new
opportunities in the market. Here are some key benefits that these types of GST offer to e-commerce
sellers:

1. Enhanced Credibility and Compliance

GST registration not only enhances the credibility of e-commerce sellers but also ensures compliance
with the law, helping to avoid penalties associated with non-compliance. This legitimacy is crucial for
building trust with customers and maintaining a good standing with tax authorities .

2. Streamlined Operations and Simplified Tax Structure

The types of GST have unified the earlier fragmented tax system into a single cohesive structure. This
simplification helps in reducing the administrative burden and costs related to managing multiple
taxes and compliances, thereby streamlining business operations. The 'one nation, one tax' approach
has facilitated smoother interstate logistics and less red tape .

3. Input Tax Credit (ITC) Benefits

GST allows e-commerce sellers to claim input tax credit on the taxes paid on inputs, capital goods,
and input services. This reduces the overall tax burden as sellers can offset this credit against their
output tax liabilities. Proper management of ITC can significantly improve cash flow and reduce the
cost of doing business, thus enhancing profitability.

4. Access to a Wider Market

By standardising tax rates across states and removing interstate barriers, the types of GST have
enabled e-commerce businesses to expand their reach across all states without worrying about
variable tax rates. This has not only helped them expand their customer base but also achieve a
more scalable and diversified business model .

Conclusion

Understanding GST is not just about meeting legal requirements; it’s a strategic advantage in the e-
commerce landscape. The various types of GST—IGST, SGST, CGST, and UTGST—are not merely tax
obligations but essential tools for smart fiscal management and business expansion.
