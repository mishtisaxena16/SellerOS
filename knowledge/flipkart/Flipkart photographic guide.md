The Top 6 Types of Product Photography

Photoshoot Representing an Individual Product

This is one of the primary and crucial types of product photography carried out with a plain
background, usually in white. It involves clicking high-definition pictures of a single product from
different angles with the right camera settings. The ideal aperture is between f/8 and f/11, and the
focal length should be between 35-60 mm on a cropped-sensor camera.

Photoshoot Demonstrating Usage of the Product

To create a realistic pitch of a product, it is ideal to take some lifestyle shots that demonstrate its
usage. For example, while doing a photoshoot of sunglasses, taking pictures of a person wearing
them might create a better impact.

Photoshoot of Multiple Products

Grouping multiple items in a single frame perfectly encapsulates the variety of products offered by
the company. Not only does it make the brand reliable, but it also increases the possibility of the
customer buying more items than they had decided.

Photoshoot Depicting the Size of the Product

Shopping online eliminates the possibility of physically checking out or testing a product. This might
create uncertainty regarding its actual size. To clear out this confusion in the customer’s mind,
placing the product with daily-use items can definitely help.

Photoshoot Emphasizing the Packaging of the Product

To let customers know how the item is packaged, it is important to show them the box or cover it
comes in. Moreover, to provide additional information, one can even take pictures of the labels on
the product. It can be reassuring and encouraging for the customer to know about its manufacturing
and constitution.

Photoshoot Emphasizing the Features of a Product

This type of product photography involves taking close-up pictures to highlight each and every
component. Taking these photos with precise zoom allows you to emphasise important features.
While uploading it on the e-commerce website, you can also show the anatomy of the product.

Equipment Required for Jewellery Product Photoshoots

To start your jewellery photography journey, you'll need a few essential pieces of equipment. Let's
take a look at each one:

Camera

While an expensive camera is not necessary, it is crucial to have one that can capture high-resolution
and clear images. Whether you choose a smartphone with a decent camera or a DSLR with a quality
lens, ensure that the camera you use can produce sharp and detailed photos of your jewellery
products. Here are a few factors to consider:

Image Quality

A good camera can produce high-resolution photos with excellent colour accuracy and depth. This is
particularly important in jewellery photography, where the smallest details and intricacies of the
piece need to be captured clearly. A high-quality image will help to showcase the piece in its best
light, making it more attractive to potential buyers.

Macro Capabilities

Many jewellery pieces are small and intricate, so a camera with the capacity to capture details is
necessary to capture close-up shots that reveal the finer details. A macro lens allows the
photographer to focus on specific parts, such as the settings, stones, or engravings, and produce
images that are sharp and clear.

Light Sensitivity

Jewellery photography often requires shooting in low-light conditions, especially when working with
delicate gemstones that may be damaged by direct sunlight. A camera with good light sensitivity can
capture high-quality images even in low-light environments, reducing the need for additional lighting
equipment.

Image stabilisation

Jewellery product shoots often involve taking pictures of small objects, which can be challenging due
to camera shake or movement. A camera with image stabilisation reduces the risk of blurry images,
ensuring that the photos are crisp and clear.

Tripod

Using a tripod is essential for achieving sharp and consistent jewellery product photos. It eliminates
camera shake, allowing you to capture clear and focused images. Invest in a sturdy tripod that can
securely hold your camera or smartphone in place during the photoshoot.

Stability

A tripod provides stability and prevents camera shake, which is particularly important in jewellery
photography, where the subjects are often small and delicate. Even the slightest movement of the
camera can cause blurry images, which can negatively impact the overall quality of the photo. A
tripod ensures that the camera remains stationary, allowing the photographer to take sharp images
easily.

Precision

Jewellery photography often requires precise positioning of the camera to capture the intricate
details of the pieces. A tripod allows the photographer to adjust the camera's height, angle, and
position with precision, ensuring that the subject is framed perfectly and that every aspect of the
jewellery is captured accurately.

Flexibility

A good tripod should offer flexibility in terms of adjustability, allowing the photographer to change
the height, angle, and orientation of the camera quickly and easily. This feature is useful when
shooting different types of jewellery, such as rings, earrings, and necklaces, which require varying
angles and perspectives.

Lighting

Lighting is crucial in jewellery photography. Natural light can work wonders, so consider setting up
your photoshoot near a window. If that is not available or sufficient, invest in artificial sources such
as softboxes or LED lights. Here is how they can help:

Highlight the Details

Jewellery pieces have intricate details that require proper lighting to be visible. Good lighting helps to
highlight the texture, cut, and clarity of the stones, as well as the intricate designs and patterns on
the metal. It brings out the smallest details that would otherwise go unnoticed, making the jewellery
look more beautiful and appealing.

Enhance Colour

Lighting can also affect the colour of the jewellery. Different light sources can bring out different
colours in the gemstones, and a skilled photographer will know how to use lighting to enhance the
natural colours of the stones. For example, daylight bulbs can bring out the blue undertones in
diamonds, while warm white bulbs can enhance the yellow and orange hues in citrines.

Add Depth and Dimension

Good lighting can add depth and dimension to the jewellery, making it appear three-dimensional
even in a two-dimensional image. By using shadow and highlight techniques, a photographer can
create a sense of volume and depth, which makes the jewellery look more realistic and attractive.

Background

Choosing the right background is important to highlight your jewellery products. A white or neutral
background is a popular choice as it allows your products to stand out and ensures a clean and
professional look. Listed below are other reasons that explain the significance of backgrounds:

Contextualisation

The background sets the scene for the jewellery, providing context and meaning to the piece. It can
help to convey the message or theme of the collection.

Brand Recall

Consistent backgrounds enable customers to connect specific product images to brands. This lends a
sense of credibility to the brand, establishing a place in the minds of shoppers.

Foam Boards

To enhance the lighting and minimise shadows, use white foam boards as reflectors. They help with:

Easy positioning

Foam boards are lightweight and easy to handle, making them ideal for positioning jewellery pieces
at different angles and heights. Photographers can quickly adjust the board's position to achieve the
desired perspective, creating visually appealing images that showcase the jewellery from various
sides.

Branding consistency

Foam boards can help create a consistent visual identity for jewellery brands, reinforcing their style
and aesthetic across different marketing materials. By using the same foam board background in
every photo shoot, photographers can create a recognisable look and feel that resonates with the
target audience and strengthens the brand's image.

Clamps

Clamps or tape are essential for securing your background and foam boards in place. They ensure
that your setup remains consistent throughout the photoshoot, preventing any unwanted movement
or adjustments. Allow us to elaborate below:

Stabilisation

Jewellery photography can often feature small and delicate and can be difficult to hold steady during
photography. Clamps help stabilise the jewellery, keeping it secure and still, which is particularly
important when using a tripod or shooting with a slow shutter speed. This ensures that the images
are sharp and clear, with no blur or movement.

Positioning

Clamps allow photographers to position the jewellery in specific ways, such as angling it to catch the
light or highlight particular features. They can also be used to elevate the jewellery off the
background, creating depth and dimension in the image. This helps create visually appealing images
that showcase the jewellery in the best possible way.

Flexibility

Clamps are highly flexible and can be adjusted to fit different sizes and shapes of jewellery. They can
be used to hold everything from small earrings to large necklaces and can be adjusted to
accommodate different thicknesses and widths. This flexibility makes them suitable for use with a
wide range of jewellery pieces, making them a versatile tool for photographers.

10 Tips for Jewellery Product Photography

Look for the Proper Surfaces

When exploring jewellery photography, it's important to consider the surfaces on which your
products will be placed. A clean and smooth surface, such as a glass or acrylic table, can create
beautiful reflections and add a touch of elegance to your photos. Experiment with different surfaces
to find the one that best compliments your jewellery pieces.

Find a Durable Jewellery Photography Tripod

Ensure that your tripod is sturdy and durable enough to hold your camera or smartphone securely.
This will prevent any accidental movement or vibrations during the jewellery photography shoot,
resulting in sharp and clear images.

Use Off-Camera Flashes for Jewellery Photography

If you're using artificial lighting, consider using off-camera flashes to create a more dynamic and
professional look. Off-camera flashes can help you control the direction and intensity of the light,
allowing you to highlight specific areas of your jewellery and create stunning visual effects.

Limit Your Reflections when it comes to Jewellery Photography

Reflections can be a challenge when photographing jewellery. To minimise unwanted reflections, use
a polarising filter on your camera lens or consider using a diffuser to soften the light. Additionally, be
mindful of the positioning of your lighting and the angle at which you're capturing the jewellery to
avoid any unwanted reflections.

Choose a Perfect Jewellery Photography Background

Selecting the right background for your jewellery product photos is crucial. A clean white or neutral
background is often preferred as it allows your products to take centre stage. However, don't be
afraid to experiment with different backgrounds to create a unique and eye-catching look that aligns
with your brand.

Contrasting Colours Features

If you want to add visual interest to your jewellery product photography, consider incorporating
contrasting colours. Pairing your jewellery with complementary backgrounds or props can create a
striking and captivating image that grabs the viewer's attention.

Get Your Jewellery Cleaned

Before you begin jewellery photography, ensure that it's clean and free from any smudges or
fingerprints. The high level of detail captured by your camera will reveal any imperfections, so take
the time to clean your jewellery thoroughly before each photoshoot.

Precision in Focus

To showcase the intricate details of your jewellery, it's crucial to have precise focus. Use manual
focus or select the appropriate autofocus point to ensure that the most important elements of your
jewellery photography are sharp and well-defined.

Position Your Lighting for Enhanced Jewellery Photography

Experiment with different lighting setups to achieve the desired effect for your jewellery product
photos. Consider using a 45-degree angle lighting setup with softboxes or umbrellas to create soft
and even lighting that highlights the details of your jewellery. Adjust the position and intensity of the
lights to eliminate any unwanted shadows or hotspots.

Retouch and Finalise Jewellery Photography Images

After capturing your jewellery product photos, it's time to retouch and finalise them. Whether you
choose to hire a professional photo editor or do it yourself, make sure to enhance your photos to
achieve a polished and professional look. Adjust the colours, sharpen the details, and remove any
imperfections to create stunning images that will captivate your customers.
