Pricing is an important ingredient while preparing a product for sale. Businesses often need help
setting the value of the different items in their catalogue as many factors are involved. To simplify the
process and offer better clarity, here is a step-by-step guide on how to price products.

This structured approach takes into account various elements such as production costs, market
demand, competitor pricing, and customer expectations. By following these steps, a business can
develop strategies for how to do pricing of a product appropriately.

This guide on how to price your product can help you strike a balance between staying competitive
and achieving the desired goals and output. So, let’s begin and look at the steps you need to take to
accurately price a product and grow your business.

6 Crucial Steps to Learn How to Set Price for a Product

Getting the price right is fundamental for your brand's financial health. This section will delve into six
crucial steps that provide a comprehensive framework for setting an effective price point. Follow

these guidelines on how to price products to make informed decisions that align with your business
goals.

Step 1: Evaluate Your Costs and Overhead Charges

Before you start setting prices, you need to understand your costs. This includes direct and indirect
costs. Direct costs are directly related to the manufacturing of your product, such as materials,
labour, and overhead expenses. Indirect costs, which are not directly related to production, include
expenses like rent, utilities, overhead, marketing, and administration.

Example: Let's say you're selling soaps.

The cost of materials like oils, lye, and fragrances sums up to ₹10 per bar.

The fixed expenses like factory rent, utilities, and salaries amount to ₹35 per bar for a month.

The variable expenses like marketing, packaging, and distribution account for ₹5 per bar.

Total cost per bar = ₹10 + ₹35 + ₹5 = ₹50.

In this example, your total cost per bar is ₹50. To determine your selling price, you'll need to factor in
how much you want to cover above your costs. This amount will help cover unexpected expenses,
reinvest in the business, or expand in the future.

Step 2: Identify Your Market's Consumers

Understanding your target market is essential for deciding how to price a product. Different
demographics vary in their price sensitivity, quality expectations, and brand loyalty. Knowing your
target audience, for instance, what price they are willing to pay, allows you to tailor your approach to
meet their specific needs and preferences.

Example: If you're selling high-end luxury handbags, your target audience may be willing to pay a
premium for superior quality, exclusivity, and brand reputation. Conversely, your target audience may
prioritise value and practicality if you're selling budget-friendly backpacks for students.

Step 3: Adjust Prices According to Market Conditions

The market is constantly evolving. Factors like economic conditions, seasonality, and competitor
actions can significantly influence both demand and supply. To stay competitive and boost revenue,
it's essential to regularly review and adjust the prices based on changing market conditions.

Example: If you're into the business of coats, you may consider increasing prices temporarily during
winter to capitalise on the increased demand. Conversely, if the economy is experiencing a dip, you
may need to lower prices to maintain sales volume. Regularly monitoring market trends and making
adjustments can optimise your product price and maximise revenue.

Step 4: Analyse Competitor Pricing

When deciding how to price your product, it's crucial to understand how your competitors set their
prices. It's important to note that you don't necessarily have to match or beat their prices, but
knowing the industry standard helps provide context for your pricing decisions.

Example: You have launched an online candle business and want to know how to price a new
product in the market. You can start by researching other e-businesses that sell similar products,

shortlist two to five competitors and analyse their pricing in terms of size, scent, and type. This will
give you an idea of where your products’ prices stand in relation to your competition.

Furthermore, determine your unique selling point. Based on your analysis, think about what sets
your candles apart from your competitors. Use this information to decide how you want to position
your product prices in the market. Finally, set the prices of your products based on the findings.

Step 6: Seasonal and Promotional Discounts

In any business, offering seasonal and promotional discounts can be an effective way to attract more
customers and increase sales. However, planning these discounts carefully is crucial to avoid affecting
your margins.

Example: You sell swimwear, offering a discount at the beginning of summer can attract more
customers. Additionally, consider offering bundle deals to incentivise purchases further.
